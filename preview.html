THE SCIENCE, SIMPLIFIED

How it works

Materials heat differently. Dark surfaces absorb more sunlight. Reflective roofs and light surfaces heat less in this model.

Shade blocks sunlight. Buildings and trees shade surfaces to their east. Taller buildings cast longer shadows; their roofs can still be hot.

Trees help nearby surfaces. Leaves release water, which can cool their surroundings. Our simplified effect fades with distance and is capped so overlapping trees cannot cool without limit.

These changes are recalculated immediately. Real temperatures also depend on wind, moisture, time, and local conditions.

Read the model, assumptions, and sources ↗
Science sources & model limits

EPA explains urban heat islands, tree shade and water release, and reflective roofs. These sources support the ideas, not our exact temperature numbers.

All numerical parameters are illustrative. We hold the weather fixed, simplify material properties, and leave out wind, changing moisture, nighttime heat, and energy use. Displayed decimals help compare changes; they do not mean measured accuracy.
